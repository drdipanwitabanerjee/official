Overview

Understanding ovarian cancer

Ovarian cancer develops in the tissue of the ovaries and is one of the five gynaecological cancers Dr. Banerjee treats, alongside cervical, uterine, vulvar and vaginal cancers. Because the ovaries sit deep in the pelvis, tumours can grow for some time before they produce symptoms specific enough to prompt an urgent work-up — which is why awareness of the early warning signs, and prompt referral to a gynaecological oncologist when they appear, matters.

Unlike cervical cancer, there is currently no routine screening test for ovarian cancer. This makes symptom awareness and timely clinical evaluation the most practical tools for early detection, alongside genetic risk assessment for women with a relevant family history.

Symptoms

Symptoms to watch for

Ovarian cancer symptoms are often subtle and easy to mistake for common digestive or menstrual issues. Persistent or worsening symptoms — rather than a single episode — are what warrant evaluation.

Persistent Bloating

Ongoing abdominal bloating or a feeling of increased abdominal size that does not settle over a few weeks.

Pelvic or Abdominal Pain

Discomfort or pain in the pelvis or abdomen that persists rather than resolving on its own.

Feeling Full Quickly

Loss of appetite or feeling full after eating only a small amount.

Urinary Changes

New or increased urinary frequency or urgency without a clear cause.

Risk Factors

Risk factors & genetic counselling

A family history of ovarian or breast cancer, or a BRCA1 or BRCA2 gene mutation, significantly increases a woman's risk of developing ovarian cancer. Because there is no routine population screening programme for this cancer, understanding personal and family risk is one of the few tools available for earlier detection.

Dr. Banerjee's team can guide patients through genetic risk assessment and counselling where relevant — helping women with a strong family history understand their options, which may include closer surveillance or risk-reducing discussions with the appropriate specialists.

Ovarian Cancer, answered directly

What are the early symptoms of ovarian cancer, and is it hereditary?

Ovarian cancer symptoms are often subtle and easy to mistake for common digestive or menstrual issues — persistent bloating, pelvic or abdominal pain, feeling full quickly after eating, and changes in urinary frequency or urgency. Because there is no routine screening test for ovarian cancer, awareness of these symptoms and prompt evaluation matter. Dr. Banerjee's team can guide patients through genetic risk assessment and counselling where relevant.

Is there a screening test for ovarian cancer?

No — unlike cervical cancer, ovarian cancer does not currently have a routine population screening test. This makes awareness of symptoms such as persistent bloating, pelvic pain, early satiety and urinary changes especially important, alongside genetic risk assessment for women with a relevant family history.

Does Dr. Banerjee perform robotic surgery for ovarian cancer?

Dr. Banerjee is fellowship-trained in robotic surgery at Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Centre, New York, and applies minimally invasive and robotic techniques for gynaecological malignancies where appropriate for the individual case, alongside open surgical approaches such as staging and cytoreductive surgery when the disease requires them.

Can fertility be preserved during ovarian cancer treatment?

For women of reproductive age with early-stage disease, fertility-sparing surgery may be an option depending on the specific case. Dr. Banerjee provides oncofertility counselling to guide patients through these options as part of her broader gynaecological oncology practice.
